**Overview.** This essay from Banco de la República, Colombia's central bank, documents gender gaps in Colombia across four areas: demography, education and poverty; the labor market; the macroeconomic effects of closing gender gaps; and policy recommendations. It covers time use and care work, the labor market effects of motherhood, female labor force participation, urban and regional wage gaps, and gender gaps in old-age economic protection.

**My contribution: Section 1.1, Gender demographic trends, 1973–2023.** Using the population censuses of 1973, 1993, 2005 and 2018 and vital statistics on deaths (1979–2023) and births (1998–2023), the section documents how Colombia's demographic transition has unfolded for men and women. The working-age population (15–65) has grown steadily since 1973, opening a demographic dividend that population ageing will gradually close. Monthly births have been declining, with a seasonal peak between August and November and a male-to-female birth ratio between 1.045 and 1.075, in line with the biological norm and with no sign of sex preferences at birth. Higher neonatal and infant mortality among boys offsets their slight majority at birth, and both rates have fallen steadily. Men outnumber women at young ages, but their share falls with age because of higher male mortality. The main causes of death are similar for both sexes, but external causes account for a much larger share of male deaths, while cancer weighs more among women.
